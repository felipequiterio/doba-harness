Hyperbolic Trajectories

Define:
  theta_inf  = the true anomaly of the (outgoing) asymptote
  v_inf      = the hyperbolic excess velocity (the speed a vehicle on an escape
               trajectory has in excess of escape speed; the speed it would
               still have "at infinity")
  delta      = the deflection angle of v_inf = the angle between the incoming
               and outgoing asymptotes = the turn angle = the amount v_inf is
               turned during the flyby
  rho        = the hyperbolic asymptote angle

The energy equation for a hyperbolic trajectory is

        v^2/2 - mu/r  =  v_inf^2 / 2  =  -mu/(2a)                    (3.24)

Solving for the semi-major axis a (note a is negative for a hyperbola):

        a = - mu / v_inf^2                                          (3.25)

Also, the periapsis radius satisfies

        r_p = a * (1 - e)                                           (3.26)

Solving this equation for e and substituting for a using Eq. (3.25):

        e = 1 - r_p/a = 1 + r_p * v_inf^2 / mu                      (3.27)

which is a function of r_p and v_inf, the fundamental parameters for flyby
analyses.

At periapsis, the energy equation gives

        v_p = sqrt( v_inf^2 + 2*mu/r_p )                            (3.28)

Determine theta_inf, delta, and rho. Each of these is a measure of the bending
of a hyperbolic trajectory. Solving the conic equation for cos(theta) and taking
the limit as r approaches infinity:

        theta_inf = arccos( -1/e )                                  (3.30)

It is geometrically obvious that

        theta_inf = delta/2 + pi/2                                  (3.31)

From Eqs. (3.30) and (3.31), we obtain

        1/e = sin( delta/2 )                                        (3.32)

Solving for delta,

        delta = 2 * arcsin( 1/e )                                   (3.33)

From this equation we see that a large value of e implies a small delta;
therefore a large e implies little bending. The shape of the hyperbolic
trajectory depends on the closest-approach distance r_p, the speed at infinity
v_inf, and the gravitational parameter mu of the planet.

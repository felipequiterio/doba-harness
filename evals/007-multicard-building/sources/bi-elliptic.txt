Bi-elliptic Transfer (three-burn transfer between two coplanar circular orbits)

A bi-elliptic transfer moves a spacecraft from an inner circular orbit of radius
r1 to an outer circular orbit of radius r2 by way of an intermediate apoapsis
radius r_b that lies beyond the outer orbit (r_b > r2). It uses two transfer
ellipses and three burns.

  - First transfer ellipse: periapsis at r1, apoapsis at r_b.
        a_t1 = (r1 + r_b) / 2
  - Second transfer ellipse: periapsis at r2, apoapsis at r_b.
        a_t2 = (r2 + r_b) / 2

The three impulsive burns are:

  Burn 1 (at r1):  inner circular speed  ->  first ellipse periapsis speed.
  Burn 2 (at r_b): first ellipse apoapsis speed -> second ellipse apoapsis speed.
  Burn 3 (at r2):  second ellipse periapsis speed -> outer circular speed.

Building-block formulas:

  - Circular-orbit speed at radius r:
        v_c = sqrt( mu / r )

  - Speed on any orbit at radius r, given semi-major axis a (vis-viva):
        v = sqrt( mu * (2/r - 1/a) )

The three burn magnitudes are the speed differences at each point:

        dv1 = v(r1,  a_t1) - v_c(r1)
        dv2 = v(r_b, a_t2) - v(r_b, a_t1)
        dv3 = v_c(r2)      - v(r2,  a_t2)

and the total cost is

        dv_total = dv1 + dv2 + dv3

where mu is the gravitational parameter, r1 the inner radius, r2 the outer
radius, and r_b the intermediate apoapsis radius. For large r2/r1 the
bi-elliptic transfer can cost less total dv than a Hohmann transfer, at the
expense of a longer transfer time.

Proving Kepler's Laws from Newton's Laws

Integrating the equations of motion over one full revolution leads to Kepler's
Equation. Setting the eccentric anomaly E = 2*pi in Kepler's Equation (one
complete orbit), we obtain the orbital period tau:

        tau = 2 * pi * sqrt( a^3 / mu )                            (2.34)

This equation proves Kepler's Third Law, which states that

        tau^2  is proportional to  (mean radius)^3

where a is the semi-major axis of the orbit and mu is the gravitational
parameter (mu = G*M) of the central body.

Def.: The mean motion (average angular velocity) is

        n = sqrt( mu / a^3 )                                       (2.35)

The mean motion is 2*pi / tau in radians per second.

(For Kepler, a = (r_p + r_a)/2, the average of the periapsis and apoapsis
radii. Reference: Prussing, 1977.)
